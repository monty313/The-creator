 The Concept 
 Markets alternate between two states: consolidation (energy storing) and trend (energy releasing). A breakout strategy waits for price to compress into a well-defined range, then enters the moment price breaks free from that range with conviction. The logic is that the longer and tighter the consolidation, the more energy stored, and the stronger the subsequent move. 

 How to Use It 

 Entry Rules 

 Identify a clear range: price must have tested the resistance level at least 2 times (and support at least 2 times) over 5–20 bars minimum. 

 The breakout candle must CLOSE above resistance — a wick through resistance does not count as a valid breakout. 

 Volume confirmation: breakout candle's volume must be notably higher than the 20-bar average volume. Volume validates the breakout is real, not a fakeout. 

 Enter at the close of the breakout candle, or on the open of the next bar if you missed the close. 

 Retest entry (lower risk): wait for price to pull back and retest the broken resistance as new support. Enter on the bounce. Miss some moves but greatly reduce fakeout risk. 

 Stop Loss and Exit 

 Stop loss: Below the low of the breakout candle, OR below the resistance level that was broken (now acting as support). 

 Target: Measure the height of the range (high − low) and project that distance upward from the breakout point. This is the measured move target. 

 If price closes back inside the range after breaking out — exit immediately. The breakout has failed. 

 Worked Example 
 NIFTY 50 has been consolidating between 22,000 (support) and 22,500 (resistance) for 12 trading days. Range height = 500 points. On day 13, a large bullish candle closes at 22,580 on 40% above-average volume. Entry at 22,580. Stop loss at 22,200 (below the range). Measured move target: 22,500 + 500 = 23,000. Target achieved over 3 weeks. 

 Common Mistakes 

 Entering on a wick breakout — price must CLOSE above the level. Wicks test levels all the time without confirmation. 

 Ignoring volume — a breakout on average or below-average volume is a major warning sign of a false break. 

 Setting the stop too tight inside the range — gives no room for the natural retest that often follows a breakout.
