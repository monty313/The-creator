STRATEGY 1 
 MOVING AVERAGE CROSSOVER 
 The Golden Cross and Death Cross — the most widely known trend signal in markets 

 Diagram 1 — Golden Cross (50 MA crosses above 200 MA → buy) and the exit when 50 MA crosses back below the 200 MA. SL at a close below 200 MA. 
 The Concept 
 A moving average smooths out price noise by averaging the last N closing prices. When a faster moving average (shorter period) crosses above a slower one (longer period), it signals that recent price action has become meaningfully more bullish than the longer-term average — the "Golden Cross." The opposite crossing is the "Death Cross." These crossovers are not predictive — they confirm that momentum has already shifted, and they signal you to join it. 

 How to Use It 

 Entry Rules 

 Long entry: The 50-period MA crosses ABOVE the 200-period MA on a daily chart. 

 Confirm with direction: Both MAs should be sloping upward at the crossover point, not one flat and one rising. 

 Optional volume filter: Volume should be above its 20-day average on the crossover bar for added conviction. 

 Enter on the next candle's open after the crossover is confirmed — do not anticipate. 

 Stop Loss and Exit 

 Initial stop loss: Place below the 200-period MA. A daily close below it invalidates the trend thesis. 

 Primary exit: The 50-period MA crosses back BELOW the 200-period MA — the Death Cross. 

 Trailing option: As the trade progresses, trail the stop to just below the 50-period MA on a daily close basis. 

 Take partial profits at 1:1 RR if desired; let the remainder run with the trailing stop. 

 Worked Example 
 A stock closes at ₹500 on the day the 50 MA crosses above the 200 MA (both MAs sloping up). You enter long at ₹502 on the next open. The 200 MA sits at ₹475 — your stop loss is a daily close below ₹475, risking ₹27 per share. Over the following 8 weeks the stock rallies to ₹680. The 50 MA has risen to ₹610. You trail your stop to a close below ₹610. The stock eventually pulls back, closes at ₹605 — exit triggered. Total capture: approximately ₹103 per share on a ₹27 risk. 

 Common Mistakes 

 Trading crossovers on intraday charts — the 50/200 crossover is a daily chart signal. On 5-min charts it generates constant false signals. 

 Entering at the first crossover in a choppy, sideways market — MA crossovers in a ranging market produce frequent whipsaws. 

 Using simple (SMA) vs exponential (EMA) MAs interchangeably — EMAs react faster; SMAs smooth more. Decide and be consistent.
